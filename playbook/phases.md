# The launch, in eight phases

Written by Omega Games. Rules marked "Steam rule", "Epic rule" and so on come from the official pages linked in `data/gates.json`. Everything else is our advice, a guideline you can adapt.

## 1. Groundwork
Until you show the game publicly.
- **Find the hook.** One sentence a stranger would repeat: what you do in the game and why it feels different.
- **Pick your player.** Not "everyone". The person who already loves games like yours.
- **Name five comparable games.** They tell you your tags, a sensible price range, and which creators and writers might care.
- **Choose a home base.** One link every post points to: usually your Steam page, otherwise your itch.io or store page. One place to count interest.
- **Set up store accounts early.** Steam rule: the Steam Direct fee is $100 per app, recouped after $1,000 adjusted gross revenue. Epic rule: $100 per product, and verification can take up to two weeks.
- **Hold the reveal until the look is settled.** Steam notes that players who follow early can be confused if the game changes a lot.

## 2. Reveal
When your store page goes public. Earlier is usually better (guideline): Steam notes little downside to a long Coming Soon period unless the game changes a lot.
- **Store page to spec.** Steam rule: capsules need a readable title, screenshots show gameplay only, and you need at least 5 of them at 1920x1080 or larger.
- **Leave review time.** Steam rule: submit the page at least 7 business days before you want it live, and it must be public for at least two weeks before release.
- **Short description.** Steam rule: plain text, a few hundred characters, nothing time-sensitive like "now available".
- **Tracking links.** Make one link per channel so your own numbers show what works. Steam reports wishlists and purchases per tracking link for signed-in visitors.
- **Website and press kit.** One page you own, one press kit link to send when anyone asks.

## 3. Grow
Roughly 36 to 12 weeks out.
- **A rhythm you can keep.** Small and steady beats bursts you cannot repeat. Pick one or two channels and show real moments from the game.
- **Playtests.** Steam Playtest runs as a separate app, so it does not touch your reviews or wishlists. Act on what testers say and tell them you did.
- **Lists, not blasts.** Build a list of creators and writers who already cover games like yours. Personal notes to the right people, never mass mail.
- **Themed events.** Steam rule: unreleased games and demos can appear in themed events when the game is primarily about the theme. Steam invites eligible games by email.
- **Small paid tests, only if you choose.** One channel, one tracking link, a hard cap. Judge it on your own numbers.

## 4. Showcase
Around your festival, usually 20 to 8 weeks out.
- **Next Fest is once per game.** Steam rule: a game can join one Next Fest, needs a public store page and a public demo, and must not release before that edition ends. Steam itself says there is no right answer on which edition to pick.
- **Deadlines are firm.** Each edition page lists its registration deadline and review dates. The coach reads them weekly.
- **Demo notice.** Steam rule: once per game, you can notify wishlisters about a free demo, within two weeks of its release.
- **QA is yours.** Steam rule: build review checks that it runs and has what you promised, not that it is bug free.

## 5. Final approach
8 to 1 weeks out.
- **Store page final pass.** Only show what is in the launch build.
- **Build review.** Steam rule: plan at least 7 business days. We suggest about three weeks so you can fix and resubmit.
- **Date lock.** Steam rule: the release date can change until two weeks before it, then it is locked.
- **Launch discount.** Steam rule: optional, set before release, 7 to 14 days, at most 40%.
- **Keys.** Steam rule: first-time developers wait three weeks after creating an app before requesting keys. Pre-release keys are for small betas and press or creators, never for sale.
- **Outreach.** One personal email per person. If someone covers a free copy, a clear disclosure is expected of them.
- **Run sheet.** Who does what, hour by hour, on launch day.

## 6. Launch window
Seven days either side of launch.
- You press the release button. Nothing releases by itself.
- Post your announcements in the same hour on your channels.
- Reply to people, fix what breaks, and say what you fixed.

## 7. First month
- Reply to reviews with thanks and specifics.
- Steam rule: no regular discount for 30 days after release (the launch discount is the exception), and seasonal sales need 30 days since release.
- Plan the first update and announce it on your store page.

## 8. Long tail
- Join seasonal and themed events that fit.
- Steam suggests easing into discounts over time.
- Keep updates coming at a pace you can hold.
- Write down what you would repeat next time.
